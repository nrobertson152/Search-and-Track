# Controls and Consoles

> Keys, mouse, displays and the seven operator consoles

## Mouse

- **Left click** selects tracks, flights and sites on the display or in any list.
- **Right click** on a track opens its orders: the nearest suitable interceptor is sent with one click (ID PASS, INTERCEPT, SHADOW…), and in battles PRIORITY marks the track for the staff. Right click on your flight for its orders. A click anywhere else closes the menu.
- On the US display, a left click on empty space places the **light gun** for track initiation.
- **Right drag** pans the display. The **wheel** zooms the display, scrolls lists and turns knobs.
- Knobs: click the left half to step down, the right half to step up.
- Hover over any control for a tooltip.

| `+` `-` | Time compression: 1×, 2×, 4×, 8×, 16×, 32× |
| `Enter` | ADVANCE: run at 96× until something needs you |
| `M` | Display mode: situation display, radar scope, network board |
| `F1` | Tracks: surveillance and identification |
| `F2` | Weapons direction: squadrons and flights |
| `F3` | Missiles: SAM batteries and BOMARC |
| `F4` | Radar network and technical |
| `F6` | Communications: teletype, voice, requests to higher authority |
| `F7` | Intelligence (in battles: the BATTLE console) |
| `F8` | Status: bases, readiness, the computer |
| `F5` `F9` | Quick save, quick load |
| `I` | Initiate a track at the light gun (US) |
| `H` | Height request on the selected track |
| `Tab` | Next track |
| `A` | Acknowledge all alerts |
| `C` | Coverage overlay; `[` `]` change its altitude |
| `,` `.` | Previous or next radar on the scope |
| `R` | Scope range |
| `Esc` | Menu: resume, save, load, manual, settings, end shift |

Time compression drops back to real time by itself when an alert arrives (from 4× up) or the instructor's card changes. ADVANCE stops on any alert, a new track, a decision or anything else that needs you. The alert slow-down can be switched off in the menu: AUTO-SLOW ON ALERTS.

## The main display

The display has three modes, on the keys under the tube or on `M`.

**Situation display / plotting board.** The computer's or the plotters' picture: tracks with identity symbols and data blocks, your flights with their predicted intercept points, radar sites, airbases, missile sites, zones and cities. The US display draws computer-generated symbols on a cyan tube. The PVO board is a gridded map lettered by hand in Russian, with time-stamped plots: own forces in red, the enemy in blue, as Soviet convention has it.

**Two rooms.** The US Direction Center has the display in the middle, the consoles on the left and the clocks and time keys above. The PVO command post is laid out differently: a tall plotting board in the middle with its keys underneath, the operator's desk on the left, and on the right a cabinet with the wall clock, the watch timer, the time keys, status lamps, a radar scope and the lines to every radar company, regiment and battalion.

**Radar scope.** The raw video of one radar, sweep by sweep, on a long-persistence tube: echoes, clutter, noise, jamming strobes, weather. Nothing is interpreted. Step between radars with `<` `>` or `,` `.`; change the range rings with RANGE.

**Network board.** A mimic diagram of the whole air defense network: radars, lines, the command post, squadrons and batteries, with their status lamps. Click a part to open its console.

Under the tube: layer keys (coverage, raw plots, zones, labels, history, range rings), the coverage altitude, brightness and focus knobs.

## The consoles

**Tracks (F1).** The track list; for the selected track, its identity keys, HEIGHT, REPORT, DROP, MERGE and CENTER, the evidence behind the recommended identity, and a coverage analysis: which radars should see it, and if not, why not.

**Weapons (F2).** Squadrons with their alert states; the scramble keys (number of aircraft, task, geometry); the flight list; and, for the selected flight, its orders: ASSIGN, task keys, manual vectors, afterburner, weapons free, control mode, forced landing and warning shots.

**Missiles (F3).** SAM batteries and BOMARC sites: readiness, missiles, lock, designation, hold fire, salvo size and re-engagement.

**Radar (F4).** Site status and settings: MTI, IFF interrogator, detection threshold, antenna rotation, frequency channel, scheduled maintenance; the radar equation for the selected site; and on the US side the AN/FSQ-7 duplex panel.

**Comms (F6).** The teletype, voice log and radio log; alerts awaiting acknowledgement; requests to higher authority.

**Intel (F7).** The intelligence summary and what is known about the threat.

**Status (F8).** Bases, aircraft, crews and readiness.

## Settings

From the title screen or the menu: difficulty (recruit, regular, veteran — see *Battles and the War*), music, sound effects, the lettering of the PVO plotting board (Russian or English), phosphor focus, and film grain on the title and briefings. Everything except the PVO plotting board is in English on both sides: consoles, orders, radio traffic, tooltips and this manual.

The music is synthesized as you play and follows the situation: quiet on a calm watch, a pulse when something is out there, drums when weapons are in the air.
