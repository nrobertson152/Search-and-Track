# Rules of Engagement and Authority

> Who may fire, at what, and who says so

## Weapons control status

Set by higher headquarters, shown on the top bar.

- **HOLD**: fire only in self-defence, or at targets higher authority has cleared.
- **TIGHT** (US): fire at targets declared HOSTILE.
- **FREE** (US): fire at anything not positively identified as friendly. Rarely granted, and dangerous.

On the PVO side, fire is permitted at a VIOLATOR the command post has cleared; everything else needs its permission.

## Asking higher authority

From the Comms console, with a track selected:

- **HOSTILE**: ask for the track to be declared hostile (violator).
- **ENGAGE**: ask for authority to engage it.
- **REPORT**: pass it up the chain.
- **SPECIAL REL.** (US): ask for release of nuclear air defense weapons.
- **RAM** (PVO): ask permission to order a ramming attack.
- Weapons status changes: WEAPONS TIGHT, FREE or HOLD.

Answers take a minute or more; Moscow takes longer than Colorado Springs. Headquarters decides on **evidence**:

- a visual identification of a foreign military aircraft;
- an active jammer;
- a hostile act;
- or a political decision made above your head, which the scenario's briefing may hint at.

"Insufficient evidence; obtain visual identification" is the most common answer in the game, and the most important lesson in it.

## Credibility

Asking to fire on something that turns out to be civil or your own costs credibility with headquarters, and the debrief records it.

## Exercise rules

In an exercise, aircraft playing the enemy are **FAKERs** (PVO: TRAINING targets) and may be engaged; the kills are simulated and scored. Everything else is real, and needs a positive identification like any other day.

## Consequences

- A **civil aircraft** shot down ends careers, starts inquiries, and makes the next morning's front page.
- **Fratricide** is investigated.
- An intruder that reaches its target, or that escapes after you had it, is a failure the briefing will have warned you about.
- Doing nothing is also a decision, and the debrief will say so.

## The alert ladder

US: DEFCON readiness and Air Defense Warning (WHITE, YELLOW, RED) are set by higher headquarters; you may request a change. PVO: combat readiness (CONSTANT, INCREASED, FULL) is ordered from above.
