# Historical Notes

> What the scenarios draw on, and how far to trust the numbers

## SAGE and Air Defense Command

The Semi-Automatic Ground Environment linked the radars of North America to direction centers built around the AN/FSQ-7, the largest computer ever built: two of them per center, one running and one on standby, in windowless four-storey blockhouses. Operators sat at situation display consoles with light guns. The first sector went live at McGuire AFB in 1958; by the early 1960s more than twenty direction centers covered the United States. Interceptors flew from alert hangars at five minutes' notice; Army Nike batteries ringed the cities and the Strategic Air Command bases; BOMARC sites stood under SAGE control. SAGE ran into the early 1980s.

## PVO Strany

The Soviet Air Defense Forces were a separate branch of the armed forces from 1954. Radar companies reported targets by telephone to the command posts, where plotters marked them on vertical glass boards with grease pencils. The Vozdukh-1 automated system began to replace telephone reporting in the 1960s. Guidance points steered interceptors on their own radars. On 1 May 1960 an S-75 battalion near Sverdlovsk shot down Francis Gary Powers's U-2. Fighters had failed to reach him, and in the confusion that followed another salvo brought down a MiG-19 sent up after him.

## The scenarios

The scenarios are inspired by history, not reconstructions of it. Names, forces and timings are simplified, and some events are hypothetical; each briefing says so.

- **Genetrix (1956).** The United States released hundreds of camera balloons to drift across the Soviet Union at high altitude.
- **May Day Overflight (1960).** The Powers U-2 flight over the Urals.
- **Sky Shield (1961).** The air defense exercise in which all civil flights in North America were grounded while SAC and the RAF attacked NORAD with jamming bombers.
- **Northern Vigil (1962).** The Cuban missile crisis seen from a northern sector: Soviet bomber probes, a jammer, the nuclear-armed alert.
- **Low Pass (1969).** A Cuban MiG-17 flew across the Florida Straits and landed at Homestead AFB without being intercepted.
- **The Kola Airliner (1978).** Korean Air Lines Flight 902 strayed over the Kola Peninsula, was fired on, and made a forced landing on a frozen lake.
- **Bering Patrol (1984)** and **Northern Shield (1986).** Bears off Alaska, AWACS, and the cruise-missile threat of the 1980s.
- **Foxhound (1986).** MiG-31 barrier patrols against the SR-71 over the Barents Sea.
- **Red Square (1987).** Mathias Rust flew a Cessna 172 from Helsinki to Moscow and landed on a bridge beside Red Square.

## About the numbers

Radar, aircraft and missile parameters come from open sources and are approximate: each entry in the data files is tagged with its provenance. Calibration anchors each radar to its documented detection range rather than to a classified specification. The data files are plain text, and you can change them.
